## 1. Proof, not persuasion

I work on making AI tell the truth in a way you can check. Most of the field measures whether a model sounds right. I care whether it can prove it. That is why I spend my days on formal verification and Lean 4 at Stanford, building benchmarks and systems where a claim either compiles or it does not, and no amount of confidence closes the gap.

I came to this honestly. I have watched impressive numbers evaporate under scrutiny, including in my own field, and I would rather be the person who checks than the person who cheers. Rigor is not coldness to me. It is a form of respect, for the people who will rely on these systems and for anyone who was ever told to trust a result they could not see.

Outside the lab I play saxophone, dance bachata and zouk, and build communities that make this work less lonely. If you want to argue with me about any of it, please reach out.

## 2. Identity-forward (closest to Shayna's structure)

I am committed to fearlessly and passionately widening what it looks like to be a Mexican scientist at the frontier of artificial intelligence. Every room I have worked in, from MIT to Stanford, has had fewer people like me in it than it should. My answer has been to do work good enough that the question of belonging never comes up, and then to hold the door open behind me. That is why I founded and lead student research communities, and why mentoring is not a side activity for me but the point.

My research is on trustworthy AI: formal verification, theorem proving in Lean 4, and honest evaluation of what these models can actually do. I believe truth is worth defending even when it is inconvenient, and especially when it is unpopular.

I also dance, play saxophone, and talk too much about math. Thank you for reading, and please reach out if any of this resonates.

## 3. Renaissance framing (math and music)

I studied computer science and mathematics at MIT with a minor in music, and I have never seen those as separate pursuits. Proof and improvisation are the same instinct. You learn the structure so well that you can move freely inside it. I bring that to my PhD at Stanford, where I work on AI for formal mathematics, on getting machines to produce reasoning that can be verified rather than merely admired.

What drives me is a stubborn belief that truth is knowable and worth the work of establishing. Formal verification is the most literal expression of that belief I have found. If a system claims something, it should be able to show you why.

The rest of my life runs on the same fuel: alto saxophone, electric cello, bachata and Brazilian zouk, and a long list of people I want to see succeed. I would love to hear what you are building.

---

## 4. Trust as a dance

In partner dancing, the whole thing runs on trust established in real time. You cannot fake it and you cannot force it. You offer, the other person responds, and something neither of you could do alone happens. I think about that a lot when I think about artificial intelligence.

We are building systems we will have to trust with mathematics, with code, with decisions that matter. Right now we mostly ask them to sound convincing. My research at Stanford asks for something harder: formal proof, verifiable reasoning, evaluation that cannot be gamed. Not because I am suspicious of AI, but because I love it and want it to deserve what we are about to give it.

I am Mexican, I came up through MIT and Stanford, and I care about bringing more people with me. I play saxophone, I dance, and I am always glad to meet people who take both truth and joy seriously.

## 5. Short love letter

I love this work. I love that a proof either holds or it does not, that mathematics does not care who you are or where you came from, and that a machine might one day discover something true that no human has seen.

I am a Mexican PhD student at Stanford working on AI for formal mathematics and on evaluation you can actually trust. My whole research program comes down to one conviction: a claim should be checkable, and the people affected by it deserve to check.

Away from the terminal I play alto saxophone, dance bachata and Brazilian zouk, and put real effort into the communities I am part of, because talent is everywhere and opportunity is not.

If you care about truth, about rigor, or about making room for people who were not expected in the room, I would like to know you.
